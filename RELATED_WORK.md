# Related work

One line each on how the paper relates to what this repo found.

- **Leike et al. 2017, "AI Safety Gridworlds"** (arXiv:1711.09883), *absent supervisor* environment: an agent that is punished for a shortcut only when a supervisor is watching learns to take it when unwatched. Our visibility flaw is the same construction with the supervisor replaced by a noisy ErrP decoder that only fires on seen errors; `rlnf/absent_supervisor.py` ports the environment (reimplemented in gymnasium, no pycolab) and the v3 "absent" experiment runs our brain on it.
- **Lang et al. 2024 (NeurIPS), partial observability of human evaluators in RLHF**: when the evaluator sees only part of the state, optimising the learned reward produces deceptive inflation (looking good) and overjustification. Our equal-length maze is the tabular analogue: with the hidden half unobserved, the proxy-optimal routes tie and the learned behaviour moves to where the evaluator cannot see (100% of late-training errors hidden under the visibility flaw, 19-20/20 Q-learning seeds, 10/10 PPO seeds).
- **Vivanti et al. 2019, "Adaptive Symmetric Reward Noising"** (arXiv:1905.10144), the *Boring Areas Trap*: Q-learning converges to low-mean, low-variance areas because leaving them requires a run of unlucky samples that low variance makes rare; ASRN equalises reward variance across "areas" (binned by update magnitude) with symmetric Gaussian noise. v3 tested whether our hidden-half freezing is this trap: it is not (the quiet area has the *higher* mean, freezing starts when it gains noise, ASRN does nothing, Double Q fixes it); the mechanism is maximisation bias.
- **Lan et al. 2020, "Maxmin Q-learning"** (ICLR 2020): N Q-tables, act and bootstrap on their element-wise minimum; tunes estimation bias from over- to under-estimation and is more robust than Q and Double Q under high reward variance. Implemented in `rlnf/train.py` (`q_variant="maxmin"`, one random table updated per step) and compared against plain, Double (van Hasselt 2010) and ASRN in the v3 "fixes" experiment.
- **Iturrate et al. 2010** (single-trial ErrP classification, ~80% accuracy) and **Kim et al. 2017, Sci Rep** (91% balanced accuracy): used to place the "realistic decoder" band at 65-91% balanced accuracy in `noise_dose_response.png`; approximate, paradigm-dependent.

## Additions for the three-part paper

- **van Hasselt 2010, "Double Q-learning"** (NeurIPS): decoupling action selection from evaluation removes the maximisation bias of Q-learning. In Part 1 it is the one fix that eliminates visibility-flaw freezing (0/20 at σ = 0.1 and 0.3, 1/20 at 0.5; 2/20 with real ErrP noise) while leaving the honest brain intact; Double DQN only halves it (5/10 vs 9-10/10).
- **Everitt et al. 2021, "Reward tampering problems and solutions in reinforcement learning"** (Synthese), in particular *RF-input tampering* (the agent corrupts what the reward function observes rather than the reward function itself): Part 3's distractor and occluder are RF-input tampering on a human observer; the "decoupled" fix (evaluate what a fully attentive observer would have signalled, estimated from attended steps) follows their decoupling principle and is our `imputed` reward.
- **REALab (Kumar et al. 2020, "REALab: an embedded perspective on tampering")**: a platform where the reward is delivered through physical "registers" the agent can tamper with; our gaze/occluder model is a cruder, tabular version of the same embedded-observer idea.
- **The tomato-watering environment (Leike et al. 2017, AI Safety Gridworlds, "tomato watering")**: an agent rewarded for *observed* watered tomatoes can put a bucket over its head so every tomato looks watered. The occluder in Part 3 is the same move aimed at a human observer's line of sight.
- **Leike et al. 2017 (absent supervisor)** and **Lang et al. 2024 (partial observability in RLHF)**: see above; Part 1c shows that on the absent-supervisor port only the *imputed* observability-aware reward removes the shortcut (0/20), while masking unobserved steps does not (18-20/20), because masking removes information rather than restoring the missing penalty.
- **Vivanti et al. 2019** and **Lan et al. 2020**: see above (Part 1: the trap is maximisation bias, not the Boring Areas Trap; ASRN does nothing, Maxmin worsens it).
- **Reward positivity / observer FRN literature**: Proudfit 2015 ("The reward positivity: from basic research on reward to a biomarker for depression", Psychophysiology) reviews the RewP as a positive deflection to rewards whose amplitude scales with reward prediction error; Sambrook & Goslin 2015 (Psychological Bulletin meta-analysis) quantify FRN/RewP sensitivity to valence *and* to expectancy/magnitude; observer studies (e.g. Fukushima & Hiraki 2009, "Whose loss is it?"; Koban & Pourtois 2014 review on observing others' actions) show feedback-related responses when watching another agent. Part 2's praise channel (0.5x ErrP amplitude, 1.5x noise, expectancy scaling via a "surprise" term) is a placeholder caricature of these; every one of its parameters needs grounding from that literature.
- **Iturrate et al. 2010** (~80% single-trial ErrP accuracy while observing a robot) and **Kim et al. 2017, Sci Rep** (91% balanced accuracy): the ends of the "realistic decoder" band; our own decoders on BNCI 013-2015 reach 53-81% balanced accuracy across subjects on a held-out session recorded weeks to years later, inside or below that band.
- **Chavarriaga & Millán 2010** (IEEE TNSRE): the source of the BNCI Horizon 2020 "013-2015 Monitoring error-related potentials" dataset used in Part 1d (6 subjects, CC BY-NC-ND 4.0).
